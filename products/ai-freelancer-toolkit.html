Quality Checklist: Making AI Output Look Human-Quality

AI gives you speed. But raw AI output is not good enough to send to a client. Use this checklist on every single deliverable before you hit send.

  • Remove AI phrases — Delete "delve," "moreover," "it's important to note," "in today's digital landscape," "game-changer," and "leverage." These scream AI.
  • Add specific details — Replace generic statements with specific numbers, names, and examples. "Businesses see results" becomes "a local bakery saw 40% more website traffic in 2 weeks."
  • Match the brand voice — Read 3-5 pieces of the client's existing content. Note their tone (casual? professional? playful?). Adjust your output to match.
  • Shorten sentences — AI writes long, winding sentences. Break them up. If a sentence has more than 20 words, split it into two.
  • Add transitions — AI often writes paragraphs that feel disconnected. Add bridges: "Here is why that matters," "Now here is the twist," "So what does this mean for you?"
  • Run a plagiarism check — Use Grammarly or Copyscape. AI can accidentally reproduce existing content. Always verify.
  • Read it out loud — If any sentence sounds awkward when spoken, rewrite it. This catches 90% of "AI-sounding" text.
  • Check formatting — Add headers, bullet points, bold text, and white space. Nobody reads a wall of text.
  • Fact-check everything — AI makes up statistics and dates. Verify every claim, every number, every name.
  • Get a second pair of eyes — If possible, have someone else read it before you send it. Fresh eyes catch what yours miss.
The 80/20 rule: AI does 80% of the work in 20% of the time. You do the remaining 20% of the work (editing, polishing, customizing) — and that is what makes it worth paying for.

Part 4: Scaling to $5K/Month

From 1 Client to 10: The Referral Machine

Getting your first client is the hardest part. Getting your next 9 is much easier — if you build a referral system. Here is how:

The 3-Touch Referral System

Touch 1: Over-Deliver on the First Project

Your first project for every client should be slightly more than what they paid for. If they ordered 3 blog posts, include a bonus outline for a 4th. If they ordered a social media calendar, include 3 bonus caption variations. This costs you 15 extra minutes and creates a "wow" moment.

Touch 2: Ask for a Review at the Right Time

Do not ask for a review when you deliver the work. Ask 3 days later, after they have had time to see the quality. Use this script:

REVIEW REQUEST Hi [NAME], I hope the [DELIVERABLE] is working well for you! If you are happy with the results, would you mind leaving a quick review on [PLATFORM]? It takes about 60 seconds and it really helps me grow my business. Here is the direct link: [LINK] Thank you so much — it was great working with you! [YOUR NAME]

Touch 3: The Referral Ask

One week after the project is done, send this message:

REFERRAL REQUEST Hi [NAME], glad you liked the work! Quick question — do you know anyone else who might need [SERVICE]? I am taking on a few new clients this month and I would love to work with people in your network. If you refer someone who becomes a client, I will give you [INCENTIVE — e.g., "a free blog post" or "20% off your next project"]. No pressure at all — just thought I would ask! [YOUR NAME]
Referral math: If every client refers just 1 person, and half of those referrals convert, you double your client base every 2-3 months. Start with 2 clients in month 1 → 3 in month 2 → 5 in month 3 → 8 in month 4 → 12 in month 5.

1. AI as your second-pass auditor

Catch errors before the claim goes out
Act as a senior medical coding auditor. Review this de-identified encounter: [documentation] + [codes billed]. Flag likely issues: under/over-coding, missing codes, modifier errors, medical-necessity / MEAT gaps. For each, give the rationale and the guideline it relates to. Be specific; don't invent codes.

Why it matters: denials and compliance failures cost providers billions. A reliable pre-bill second pass is genuinely a business — it's the tool I'm building in public.

2. Instant guideline & code lookup

Stop digging through manuals
I'm coding [scenario]. Based on [paste the relevant guideline text], which code(s) apply, and what documentation must be present to support them? Cite the guideline you used.

Pro tip: always give it the actual guideline text (this is "RAG"). Don't trust the model's memory of coding rules — feed it the source.

3. Draft audit findings & coder education

Turn a 2-hour write-up into 10 minutes
Turn these audit findings into a clear, professional report for the coder: [findings]. Lead with patterns, not nitpicks. Include a short teaching note for each issue so it doesn't repeat. Supportive tone.

Pro tip: this is the skill that makes auditors valuable — let AI handle the writing, you supply the judgment on what matters.

4. Summarize documentation for medical necessity

Find the support (or the gap) fast
Summarize this de-identified clinical note for medical-necessity review: what diagnoses are supported, what's documented vs. coded, and what's missing to defend the codes billed.

Pro tip: great for spotting the gap between what's documented and what's coded — the #1 denial driver.
